# Global Government Budget Analysis (1946-2026)

An exploratory data analysis of 80 years of national budget data across 45 countries, covering defense, healthcare, education, infrastructure and social welfare and debt servicing.

## Project Overview

Government budgets are one of the clearest signal of any nation's priorities. The main purpose of this project is to answer a core question is that how nations have evolved their spending over the span of 80 years and how did 2008 financial crisis reshape it all and how it has impacted certain top economically powerful countries.

This dataset has records from 1946 to 2026 of over 45 countries and 9 spending categories: Defense, Education, Health, Infrastructure, Agriculture, State Transfers, Social Welfare, Interest Payments and Adminstration.

## Objectives

-Clean and preprocess a large multi decade fiscal dataset with messy and missing data handling challenges
-Identify trends across countries and regions
-Quantify the impact of 2008 financial crisis and how it impacted budget priority of several countries
-Visualizes and communicates these findings through visual charts.

## Data cleaning highlights

-Ensured each country-year's category percentages sum to ~100%
-Countries like Iran, Singapore and UAE reported 0% interest payments for every year which is impossible and flagged.

## Key findings

1. USA: has kept their defense spending extremely stable at approximately 9 to 10% of total budget despite  wars, recessions, and political shifts.
2. Russia  had a negative correlation between their spending. As defense grew - education fell; couldn't fund both
3. The 2008 crisis: countries like China, Greece, Spain increased their education and health and countries like USA, Germany etc. kept their budget allocation to defense as stable.
